# Custom Access Studios — working brief

## Current direction: advocacy and ongoing partnership

Keith explicitly rejected the previous version's transactional referral-service framing and excessive emphasis on Emily's individual role. CAS is the brand and the customer's partner throughout their accessibility journey—not just a connection between a problem and a supplier.

CAS begins with people's actual concerns, routines, priorities and needs. Its intended role is to be on their side when product pitches leave them uncertain about suitability, quality and trustworthy claims. Explain useful scrutiny without claiming certification, guaranteed scam detection, financial independence or that all sellers are dishonest.

Understand why something is difficult before proposing an answer. Explore related challenges and consider emerging needs with the person, without diagnosing, predicting decline or encouraging unnecessary purchases. For example, a difficult door might involve grip/turning, pushing force or mobility-device clearance. Windows, refrigerators and cupboards might reveal related difficulties. These are questions to explore, not clinical assessments.

Solutions may be off the shelf, adjustments to what is already there, or custom designed with appropriate specialists. Do not assume every product can adapt indefinitely. As situations and needs change, CAS can revisit what works, help adjust the approach and explore the next solution. Partnership is not a promise of unlimited support, a subscription, automatic follow-ups or a service package whose terms have not been established.

## Audience and voice

People navigating accessibility needs at home and those helping someone they care about. Lead with autonomy, choice, practical help, dignity, trust and familiar life. CAS first; Emily's legitimate founder background may support credibility but should not dominate the service narrative. Warm, concrete language—not a transactional funnel, product catalog or tool-first proposition. Do not stereotype disability or assume every visitor is older.

Mug, light and door examples remain useful, but must explicitly demonstrate how CAS thinks and listens, not appear to define the available services. Show underlying difficulty, related needs, considered options and the ability to revisit decisions. The optional private notepad supports reflection; it is not the primary point of the site.

## Scope and truthfulness

Business is still a concept. No verified public email, phone, service area, pricing, qualifications or portfolio. Do not position CAS/Emily as licensed clinicians, engineers, architects or in-house fabricators. Do not invent testimonials, actual client journeys, guaranteed outcomes, commercial independence, product testing or credentials. Clearly label illustrative situations.

Static GitHub Pages site in secondkeith/custom-access-studios; customaccessstudios.com must not be configured without instruction. Preserve concept notice, noindex and inquiries-not-open state. No fake inquiry form. No third-party trackers or note transmission. Keep editable note, replacement confirmation, review-before-export, copy/download/print, keyboard and no-JavaScript safeguards.

## Design and acceptance

User authorizes substantive redesign and the same iteration loop as before: build, test, candid independent rendered review, address material findings, fresh review, publish accepted concept and verify live. Preserve previous versions in Git history.

Acceptance has TWO gates: (1) faithfulness to the customer-advocacy, broader-needs and ongoing-partnership vision above; (2) distinctive, coherent, award-caliber concept craft across desktop/mobile and useful interaction. An attractive referral funnel fails gate one. Independent AI opinion is not an external award, launch approval or accessibility certification. Do not shop for approval or coach reviewers into saying yes.

Gemini imagery remains optional: detailed user-facing briefs, explicitly illustrative generated scenes, no fabricated completed-project photos or fictional founder portraits.
