Art direction / Gemini prompts

Homes, not showrooms.
People, not props.

These briefs are for Keith to use in Gemini. Only the first image is needed to replace the current temporary atmosphere photograph. The others offer options for the next iteration. Generated scenes must be labeled illustrative, never presented as Emily’s completed work or real clients.

01 / Hero: room for a familiar life

Delivery: portrait 4:5, at least 2400 × 3000 pixels. Also export landscape 3:2. Keep key details in the central 60% for the site’s arched crop. No text or logos.

Create a naturalistic editorial architectural photograph for a thoughtful home-accessibility guidance studio. Show a warm, believable, modestly sized home, not a mansion or a showroom. Camera at seated eye level, approximately 1.2 metres high, with a natural 35mm lens perspective and straight verticals. In the foreground, a wide, unobstructed area of matte oak flooring leads through an open, flush doorway into a softly sunlit living space. One comfortable chair with supportive arms and a firm-looking seat; a reading light with a large, reachable control; a side table positioned within reach without blocking circulation; a few well-loved books, a handmade ceramic mug, and personal art. No loose rugs, trailing cables, raised thresholds, or decorative objects in walking routes. Muted olive, oatmeal linen, warm oak, aged terracotta, and very restrained dark green. Late-afternoon side light with soft, realistic shadows; gentle grain and true-to-life textures, no HDR, no orange filter. Leave breathing room at the upper centre for an arched crop. The room should feel individual, quietly beautiful, and lived in. Do not depict medical equipment as a symbol, hospital furniture, wall text, impossible joinery, excessive indoor plants, luxury staging, or futuristic smart-home graphics. This is an illustrative residential scene, not a certified accessible design.

02 / The connection: a conversation, not a sales pitch

Delivery: landscape 3:2, at least 3000 × 2000 pixels. Optional future approach-section image. Do not use as Emily’s portrait.

Create a candid editorial photograph of two adults discussing how to make everyday life easier in a real home. One is a homeowner in their late sixties; the other is an adult advisor. Both are seated at equal eye level at a simple oak table. The homeowner is actively pointing to a rough room sketch, clearly leading the conversation; the advisor is listening attentively. No posed handshake, no pointing at a laptop, no clipboard-as-authority, no medical clothing, no patronizing expressions. On the table: a notebook with indistinct unreadable marks, two simple material samples, and a plain lever handle sample. Warm natural window light, oatmeal and deep olive clothing, lived-in background, natural skin texture and realistic hands. Camera framed from the side at medium distance with a 50mm editorial documentary feel. Thoughtful, unhurried, respectful, not advertising smiles. Leave negative space at the left edge for possible cropping, but include no text, logos or interface overlays. These are fictional illustrative people, not real customers or staff.

03 / Thoughtfully chosen: a tactile study

Delivery: landscape 4:3, at least 2400 × 1800 pixels. Optional supporting image; not a real product recommendation.

Photograph a small, considered arrangement of everyday home fittings on warm off-white paper beside an oak offcut: a well-made satin bronze lever handle with a gently rounded end, one oversized unbranded rocker-switch sample, a warm-grey textured material sample, and a pencil. Realistically manufactured objects with plausible dimensions and mounting details. Compose asymmetrically with generous empty space, as a design editor would photograph materials rather than an ecommerce grid. Diffuse morning light from upper left, soft shadows, subtle paper texture, muted brass and charcoal accents. Close-up with a 50mm lens perspective, crisp but not excessively sharpened. No text, trademarks, clinical equipment, glossy plastic, futuristic gadgets, collaged labels, impossible mechanical details, or decorative greenery. The message is considered selection and everyday usability. Do not imply the objects are tested or endorsed products.

What to send back

Send the highest-resolution files rather than screenshots, ideally two or three hero variations. We’ll select the strongest composition, check visual plausibility, crop for desktop and mobile, and write accurate alt text. An attractive image is not evidence that a room is accessible or that a product works.

The strongest final trust image would be an authentic photo of Emily, used with her permission. We should not generate a fictional founder portrait.
